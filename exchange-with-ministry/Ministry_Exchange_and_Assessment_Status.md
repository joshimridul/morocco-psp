# Ministry Exchange, Item-Map Deliverables, and Current Assessment-Development Status

## 1. Purpose of this document

This document records the assessment-development exchange between the research team and the Moroccan Ministry team, the files that were sent and received, the requirements communicated in the email thread, the work actually completed in the returned workbook, the gaps identified in the subsequent audit, and the implications for the next empirical item and anchor analysis.

It is intended to serve four purposes:

1. preserve a clear project history so that later analysts do not have to reconstruct the exchange from scattered emails;
2. distinguish **what was explicitly requested**, **what was stated in email updates**, and **what is actually present in the supplied files**;
3. give Codex and human reviewers a reliable starting point for the historical item-response analysis; and
4. prevent unresolved assumptions about item retention, anchors, grade shifting, scoring, and pilot readiness from becoming embedded in later instruments.

This is not a judgment of the student-facing assessments themselves. At the point of the initial audit, the available evidence consisted mainly of item maps and correspondence. The actual forms, original-language items, response data, keys, rubrics, administration materials, and historical psychometric evidence were not part of that first review. Those materials are now expected to support the next phase.

## 2. Executive summary

The research team sent two Excel workbooks—a baseline item map and an endline item map—and requested a coordinated update for the 2025/2026 assessment cycle. The request had several components:

- use the existing Grade 1 baseline as the reference for the new Grade 1 baseline;
- use prior endline Grades 1–5 as the reference for the next baseline Grades 2–6, shifting each source upward by one grade;
- add a content-domain and cognitive-domain classification for every assessment question;
- comment item by item on which questions should remain, change, or be replaced;
- preserve a subset of questions unchanged across years; and
- provide the revised materials early enough for a further technical review and instrument development.

The file observed as the Ministry team’s return was `Endline item map_from_btihaj.xlsx`. Relative to the original endline workbook, it contained **836 changed cells**, all in the content-domain or cognitive-domain columns of the three subject-specific sheets. No other item-map fields changed. The consolidated `All` sheet was unchanged.

The returned workbook therefore represents a **partial domain-classification pass**, not a completed update of the baseline and endline design. It did not provide a revised baseline map, did not operationalize the one-grade shift, did not add item-level retain/change/replace recommendations, did not document a new anchor review, and did not create separate French grade maps.

Even the domain-classification task was incomplete:

- 418 of 519 subject-sheet rows had both content and cognitive domains: **80.5%**;
- Arabic: 114 of 214 rows coded: **53.3%**;
- French: 134 of 134 rows coded: **100%**;
- mathematics: 170 of 171 rows coded: **99.4%**;
- consolidated `All` sheet: 0 of 519 rows coded: **0%**.

The audit also identified coding inconsistencies, probable grade-label errors, an unsynchronized consolidated sheet, ambiguous French grade progression, and an anchor field that does not distinguish the different links the project may require.

The most important next step is to combine the item maps with the historical item-level response data and the actual administered items. That will allow the team to distinguish:

- items that function poorly and should be revised or retired;
- items that are useful scored items but unsuitable as anchors;
- items that are substantively and empirically strong anchor candidates for a particular link; and
- areas where new items are needed to meet curriculum and grade-specific blueprint requirements.

The returned workbook alone was not sufficient to freeze a revised longitudinal assessment. Historical data and actual-item review can now convert the structural audit into evidence-based advice to the Ministry.

The item maps contain English summaries and administrative metadata. ## 4. Assessment-development context

The collaboration concerns assessments for students in Grades 1–6 in:

- Arabic;
- French; and
- mathematics.

The maps are being updated in connection with Ministry field experiments and the 2025/2026 data-collection cycle. The assessments may serve several purposes that must be kept distinct:

1. measure learning outcomes in experimental impact evaluations;
2. improve precision by supplying baseline achievement covariates;
3. support baseline-to-endline comparisons within a grade or cohort;
4. support adjacent-grade or vertical comparisons;
5. support trends across school years; and
6. support exploratory or pre-specified heterogeneity analyses by content or cognitive domain.

These purposes do not impose identical measurement requirements. In particular, a baseline used only as a prognostic covariate in an RCT does not necessarily have to lie on the same vertical scale as the endline. A baseline intended to support interpretable individual growth or a common proficiency scale requires stronger linking and construct-continuity evidence.

The correspondence did not explicitly resolve which uses are primary. That decision should be documented before final anchor and scoring recommendations are made.

### 11.5 Repeated items receive inconsistent classifications

Examples from the returned French map include:

- `f21`, asking a student’s age, classified as `PO` in Grade 1, `CO` in Grades 2–3, and `PO` in Grades 4–6;
- `f23`, asking the current day, classified as `PO`, then `CO`, then `PO`;

Examples from Arabic include identical sentence-ordering tasks, such as `a324y3_nv3` and `a261_nv`, moving from `SYNT` to `ANALYSE` across grades without an explicit grade-relative coding rule.

The same item should ordinarily be coded once in a master bank and propagated to forms. If classification is intentionally grade-relative, that rule needs to be defined, justified, and consistently applied.

### 11.6 Mathematics mixes content and process categories

The mathematics content field includes labels for number/calculation, geometry/measurement, data, algebraic thinking, and `problem solving`. Problem solving is ordinarily a cognitive process or task context rather than a mutually exclusive content strand. The current map therefore mixes different dimensions.

The returned labels also contain variants such as:

- `CALCUL`, `calcul`, and `calcul `;
- `DATA` and `Data`;
- `NUMERACY` and `numeracy`;
- `REASON` and `REASO`; and
- alternative spellings of algebraic thinking.

A controlled vocabulary and data validation are needed.

### 11.7 Spreadsheet rows are not a common measurement unit

Rows in the maps can represent:

- independent questions;
- subquestions;
- passage-based questions;
- parts of multipart mathematics tasks;
- oral prompts;
- writing prompts; or
- separate rubric criteria such as fluency, cohesion, accuracy, or handwriting.

Therefore, a row count is not equivalent to:

- an independent item count;
- a score-point count;
- a test-duration weight; or
- a quantity of independent psychometric information.

Future item-bank data should add:

- `unit_type`;
- `stimulus_id` or `testlet_id`;
- `response_format`;
- `max_score`;
- `scoring_method`;
- `answer_key` or `rubric_id`;
- `rater_required`; and
- expected administration time.

Blueprint coverage should be summarized by score points and independent tasks, not only rows.

### 13.2 Map-level comparison with the baseline

Among the 209 currently marked rows:

- 27 had the same ID but different mapped wording;
- 6 had matching text under a different ID; and
- 5 had no baseline-map match identified.

Thus, 38 currently marked anchor rows did not have an exact same-ID and same-map-text match. Map matching is only an initial screen; actual forms, options, stimuli, keys, rubrics, and administration conditions must be checked.

One notable example is Arabic item `a414y3_nv3`, whose mapped sequence changes from `3-4-2-1` to `3-4-1-2`. If that difference reflects the keyed response or stimulus order rather than a transcription error, it is a substantive version change.

### 13.3 One binary anchor field is insufficient

The project may require at least three distinct links:

1. **within-grade baseline-to-endline linking**;
2. **adjacent-grade vertical linking**; and
3. **across-year trend linking**.

An item can be suitable for one link and unsuitable for another. The item bank should therefore include:

- `anchor_role`;
- `anchor_set_id`;
- `source_form`;
- `target_form`;
- `source_wave`;
- `target_wave`;
- `item_version`;
- `exposure_status`;
- an unchanged-components checklist; and
- an anchor-selection rationale.

### 13.4 A good item is not automatically a good anchor

A strong scored item may be inappropriate as an anchor if:

- its wording, stimulus, options, scoring, or administration changed;
- it is too easy or too difficult in one of the linked populations;
- it displays parameter drift or differential item functioning;
- it is unusually sensitive to repeated exposure; or
- it represents a narrow or intervention-specific response process.

Conversely, a moderately discriminating but stable and representative item may be useful as an anchor.

Final recommendations should therefore use separate classifications such as:

- strong anchor for a specified link;
- provisional anchor requiring confirmation;
- retain as a scored non-anchor item;
- retain for diagnostic or curriculum coverage only;
- revise and assign a new version; or
- replace or retire.

## 14. Domain-level heterogeneity analyses

The original email requested content and cognitive labels partly to enable heterogeneity analyses by assessed ability. Adding labels is necessary, but it does not by itself create a defensible domain outcome.

A domain score requires:

- a defined construct;
- enough independent tasks and score points;
- adequate difficulty coverage;
- sufficient reliability or test information;
- comparable representation across baseline and endline;
- stable item functioning across waves and treatment groups; and
- a pre-specified scoring and multiplicity plan.

Sparse or locally dependent domains should not be reported as separate abilities merely because a label exists in the map. The historical response analysis should determine which proposed domains support confirmatory scores, which can support exploratory analysis only, and which should be merged or omitted.

For the field experiments, item selection must not be driven by which items yield favorable treatment effects. Initial item-quality and anchor screening should be performed using pre-treatment data, previous control cohorts, or treatment-blinded data where feasible. Treatment-related item-function analyses should occur only after provisional scoring and anchor rules are frozen.

## 15. Process and governance insights from the email thread

### 15.1 Scope mismatch

The original request included classification, item decisions, preserved items, anchors, and grade shifting. The follow-up status email described the domains as completed and focused future revisions on exercise types and passages. This indicates that different participants may have been using different definitions of completion.

A written deliverable checklist with owners, deadlines, and acceptance criteria is needed.

### 15.2 Anchor sequencing

The plan to revise language passages and associated questions can conflict with the need to preserve anchors. Candidate anchor components should be reviewed and locked before substantive rewriting.

An item ID should not be preserved while its passage, image, options, key, rubric, or administration changes and then be treated as the same anchor.

### 15.3 Timeline compression

The internal sequence of feedback by August 4, stabilization by August 7, and pilot during the week of August 17 leaves little time for:

- correcting and synchronizing the maps;
- agreeing on constructs and domains;
- writing dedicated French grade forms;
- developing or revising items;
- linguistic and curriculum review;
- programming and scoring QA;
- cognitive interviewing;
- rater preparation; and
- empirical pilot analysis.

Under a compressed schedule, a minimal revision of previously functioning forms may be safer than a large redesign that cannot be properly reviewed. A rapid administration can serve as a developmental pilot, but it should not be described as validation of a final instrument.

### 15.4 “Stabilized,” “pilot-ready,” and “field-ready” are different statuses

The correspondence uses `stabilized` without an explicit acceptance standard. The project should distinguish:

1. **map complete**: required metadata, mappings, and item decisions are present;
2. **draft form complete**: all items, stimuli, options, keys, and rubrics exist;
3. **content-reviewed**: curriculum, language, fairness, and formatting reviews are resolved;
4. **pilot-ready**: administration, data capture, timing, and analysis plans are ready;
5. **empirically reviewed**: pilot item statistics and response-process findings have been evaluated; and
6. **field-ready**: the final version, scoring code, anchors, and documentation are locked.

A deadline alone does not establish any of these statuses.

### 15.5 Pilot purpose was not specified

A pilot can be designed to test:

- item difficulty and discrimination;
- students’ interpretation and response processes;
- timing and fatigue;
- administration logistics;
- digital-platform behavior;
- scoring and rater consistency; or
- all of these.

The required sample, data fields, and decision rules depend on the purpose. A written pilot protocol is needed.

### 15.6 Decision rights were unclear

The thread names reviewers and coordinators but does not identify the final technical approver for:

- the blueprint;
- item retention;
- anchor sets;
- scoring;
- pilot acceptance; or
- field readiness.

Without explicit decision rights, deadline pressure may substitute for evidence.

### 15.7 Version control was insufficient

No item-version field, form manifest, locked anchor set, or change log was visible. Every administered form should be reproducible exactly, including item order, stimuli, keys, rubrics, and scoring rules.

### 15.8 Internal and external communication should be separated clearly

The internal loop explicitly excluded MENPS. It is useful as a record of internal planning, but its assumptions and fallback options should not be treated as agreed Ministry decisions. After internal alignment, the team should issue one consolidated external technical note stating decisions, responsibilities, and unresolved questions.

## 17. Limitations of the structural audit

The audit could evaluate:

- whether the requested fields and design steps were delivered;
- workbook completeness and consistency;
- map-level wording and metadata drift;
- obvious classification inconsistencies;
- grade-shift feasibility; and
- preliminary anchor eligibility from the maps.

It could not establish:

- whether the original-language wording is valid and age appropriate;
- whether images, passages, and options are equivalent across forms;
- whether answer keys and rubrics are correct;
- curriculum alignment at the objective level;
- item difficulty and discrimination;
- distractor functioning;
- floor and ceiling effects;
- test reliability or conditional score precision;
- local dependence and testlet effects;
- rater consistency;
- item parameter drift;
- differential item functioning;
- linking error; or
- whether the proposed domains support reliable heterogeneity outcomes.

The historical item-level data and actual items are therefore not supplementary. They are the evidence required to complete the review.

## 18. Next phase: empirical item and anchor analysis

The next phase should combine four evidence streams:

1. **actual item content**: prompts, passages, images, options, keys, rubrics, and administration conditions;
2. **item-map metadata**: grade, form, domain, curriculum mapping, and existing anchor status;
3. **historical response data**: responses, scores, missingness, timing, raters, waves, grades, and clusters; and
4. **substantive review**: curriculum importance, linguistic quality, fairness, and feasibility.

### 18.1 Data readiness and score reconstruction

Before fitting psychometric models:

- inventory all files and variables;
- identify canonical student, administration, form, item-version, stimulus, and rater keys;
- distinguish omitted, not-reached, invalid, and not-administered responses;
- identify duplicate or conflicting IDs;
- verify score ranges and maximum points;
- reconstruct all reported total and subscale scores; and
- build an item-version crosswalk across waves, grades, and forms.

### 18.2 Item-quality analysis

For each item version within grade, form, and wave, examine:

- valid sample size;
- missing, omitted, and not-reached rates;
- difficulty or mean score;
- corrected item-rest association;
- distractor functioning;
- response time;
- category functioning for partial-credit items;
- rater agreement and severity for performance tasks;
- stability across schools and cohorts;
- local dependence and testlet effects; and
- contribution to score precision.

### 18.3 Anchor analysis

For each intended link:

- verify exact operational equivalence;
- examine performance in both source and target populations;
- assess parameter stability, drift, and DIF;
- check repeated-exposure risk;
- use iterative anchor purification;
- compare alternate substantively balanced anchor sets; and
- validate conclusions in another cohort, wave, or held-out group of schools where possible.

### 18.4 Candidate-form comparison

Compare at least:

- the existing form;
- a minimally revised form preserving defensible anchors; and
- a redesigned grade-specific form.

Evaluate each against:

- curriculum coverage;
- content and cognitive score-point targets;
- number of independent tasks;
- difficulty distribution;
- measurement precision;
- floor and ceiling risk;
- duration;
- oral and rater burden;
- anchor coverage;
- repeated-item exposure; and
- expected precision of the planned experimental estimand.

### 18.5 Final recommendation categories

Every item version should receive one of the following evidence-based decisions:

- strong anchor for a specified link;
- provisional anchor requiring confirmation;
- retain as a scored item but not an anchor;
- retain for diagnostic or curriculum coverage only;
- revise and create a new item version;
- replace or retire; or
- insufficient evidence.

The rationale should distinguish empirical evidence, substantive judgment, and unresolved uncertainty.

## 19. Decisions that must be resolved before final modeling

The analysis should not silently infer the following:

1. Is the baseline primarily a covariate for the experiment, a growth measure, or both?
2. Which links are truly required: within-grade pre/post, adjacent-grade vertical, across-year trend, or all three?
3. Were the same students exposed to the same items in consecutive waves?
4. Were correct answers or feedback shown after prior administrations?
5. What are the primary subject-level outcomes?
6. Which domain-level outcomes are confirmatory, and which are exploratory?
7. What are the official curriculum objectives and target weights by grade?
8. What is the maximum acceptable assessment duration by subject and grade?
9. What oral, writing, or rater burden is feasible?
10. Will French have fully separate forms for Grades 1–6?
11. What pilot purpose and item-retention rules have been approved?
12. Who has final technical and Ministry sign-off authority?
13. What calendar year corresponds to the supplied July and August deadlines?

Unresolved questions should be logged rather than answered by assumption.

## Appendix C. Terminology for later decisions

- **Item quality:** how well an item measures its intended skill in a particular form, grade, and population.
- **Anchor eligibility:** whether an operationally unchanged item is sufficiently stable and representative to support a specified score link.
- **Anchor role:** the exact linking relationship for which an item is proposed, such as within-grade pre/post, adjacent-grade vertical, or across-year trend.
- **Item version:** a unique combination of prompt, stimulus, options, key or rubric, score structure, administration, and relevant exposure history.
- **Testlet:** a group of items sharing a passage, image, oral task, or other common stimulus.
- **Strong anchor:** an item verified as operationally equivalent, substantively representative, empirically stable, and suitable for one specified link.
- **Provisional anchor:** an item that appears eligible but lacks sufficient confirmation or validation.
- **Scored non-anchor item:** an item worth including in the score but unsuitable for linking.
- **Diagnostic-only item:** an item retained to describe mastery of a specific skill but not relied on for the primary score or link.
- **Field-ready:** a form whose content, administration, scoring, versioning, and measurement evidence meet pre-specified acceptance criteria—not merely a form completed by a deadline.
